# White Blood Cell Guide

Use the reference images in `assets/` alongside these characteristics.
The `videos/` directory also contains the source project's WBC identification
video and its English transcript.

## Neutrophil

- Usually two to three times larger than a red blood cell.
- Light pink cytoplasm with small granules and occasional vacuoles.
- Dark purple nucleus with multiple connected lobes.
- Some have a C- or U-shaped nucleus.

## Lymphocyte

- Nucleus is only slightly larger than a red blood cell.
- Light, often blue, clear cytoplasm without granules.
- Rounded dark-purple nucleus fills most of the cell.

## Monocyte

- Usually two to three times larger than a red blood cell.
- Light, often blue, clear cytoplasm without granules; may have vacuoles.
- Large irregular, C-shaped, or bean-shaped dark-purple nucleus.

## Eosinophil

- Usually two to three times larger than a red blood cell.
- Darker red or purple cytoplasm with medium-sized granules.
- Dark-purple nucleus with two or three rounded connected lobes.

## Basophil

- Usually two to three times larger than a red blood cell.
- Large dark-purple granules fill the cytoplasm.
- The bean-shaped nucleus is mostly hidden by granules.

## Do Not Mark

- Red blood cells.
- Platelets, including unusually large platelets.
- Burst cells that cannot still be confidently identified.
- Red cells with platelets or debris on top.
- Overstained red cells.
- General debris or cells too obscured by debris to identify.

Staining colors vary between slides. Make the best morphology-based
classification rather than relying on one exact color.
